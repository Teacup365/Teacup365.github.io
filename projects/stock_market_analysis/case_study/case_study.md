# S&P 500 Historical Market Performance Analysis (1927–Present)
## Project Overview

The purpose of this project was to analyze nearly 100 years of historical S&P 500 market data to identify long-term performance trends, evaluate market volatility, and examine the impact of major economic events on investor behavior. Using Python, Pandas, Matplotlib, and exploratory data analysis techniques, the project transformed raw market data into actionable insights regarding risk, market resilience, and historical recovery patterns.

The analysis focused on stock price movements, daily returns, trading volume, moving averages, volatility measurements, and major market disruptions including the Great Depression, the 2008 Financial Crisis, and the COVID-19 market crash.


## Business Objectives

The project was designed to answer the following key questions:

- How has the S&P 500 performed over the long term?
- Which historical periods experienced the strongest and weakest market performance?
- When did investors exhibit the highest levels of trading activity?
- Which market events generated the greatest volatility?
- How do major financial crises impact market behavior and recovery timelines?
- What insights can historical market performance provide regarding risk and resilience?


## Methodology

Data Collection

The analysis utilized historical S&P 500 market data containing:

- Date
- Open Price
- High Price
- Low Price
- Close Price
- Trading Volume

The dataset spans approximately one century of market activity, providing a comprehensive view of market behavior across multiple economic cycles.


## Data Preparation

Data preparation included:

- Converting date fields into datetime format
- Creating Year and Month variables
- Calculating Daily Return percentages
- Creating 20-day, 50-day, and 200-day moving averages
- Calculating 30-day rolling volatility metrics
- Validating data quality and identifying missing values


## Analytical Techniques

The analysis incorporated:

- Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
- Trend Analysis
- Time-Series Analysis
- Volatility Analysis
- Moving Average Analysis
- Market Event Investigation
- Historical Recovery Analysis


## Key Findings

1. Long-Term Market Growth

Despite experiencing numerous economic recessions, wars, financial crises, and periods of uncertainty, the S&P 500 demonstrated a strong long-term upward trend throughout the dataset. Historical data shows that markets have consistently recovered from major disruptions and continued generating long-term growth.


2. Best and Worst Performing Years

The strongest annual performance occurred in 1933, with an average daily return of approximately 0.20%. This period represented a significant recovery following the most severe stages of the Great Depression.

The weakest annual performance occurred in 1931, with an average daily return of approximately -0.22%, reflecting the severity of economic contraction during the Great Depression.


3. Highest Trading Activity Occurred During the 2008 Financial Crisis

The highest trading volume in the dataset occurred on October 10, 2008, when daily trading volume exceeded 11.4 billion shares.

This extraordinary level of activity reflected heightened investor uncertainty and widespread reactions to rapidly changing financial conditions during the Global Financial Crisis.


4. Extreme Volatility Followed the 1929 Market Crash

The highest rolling volatility measurement occurred on November 19, 1929.

This period immediately followed the historic stock market crash and demonstrated the intense uncertainty that characterized the onset of the Great Depression. Elevated volatility persisted as investors reacted to rapidly deteriorating economic conditions.


5. Moving Averages Successfully Identified Market Cycles

Short-term and long-term moving averages highlighted major market transitions, including periods of expansion, contraction, and recovery.

Sustained movement below long-term moving averages frequently coincided with broader economic downturns, while upward trend reversals often signaled the beginning of recovery periods.


6. Market Recovery Patterns Differ Across Crises

The 2008 Financial Crisis generated a prolonged decline and extended recovery period.

By comparison, the COVID-19 market crash produced a sharp decline followed by a significantly faster recovery, demonstrating how policy responses, investor confidence, and economic conditions influence recovery timelines.


## Business Implications

This analysis demonstrates that while market volatility can increase substantially during periods of economic stress, long-term market performance has historically remained resilient.

The findings reinforce several important principles:

- Market downturns are inevitable but historically temporary.
- Volatility often serves as a leading indicator of investor uncertainty.
- Trading volume increases significantly during periods of market stress.
- Long-term trend analysis provides valuable context beyond short-term market fluctuations.
- Historical performance data can improve understanding of risk management and investment decision-making.

For financial analysts, investors, and business decision-makers, these insights provide a framework for evaluating market conditions and understanding how markets have historically responded to major economic events.
